title: Rumble
description: Haptic feedback on the Miniloong Pocket 1. Feel the interface as you move through it, and let games drive the rumble motor.
---

The Pocket 1 has a rumble motor. Leaf uses it for short taps as you move through
the interface, and games can drive it themselves.

## Turning it on

Go to **Settings > Hotkeys & Rumble**. Two switches share one strength, so you can
have either without the other.

- **UI Rumble** - taps from the interface: one each time the cursor moves, plus the
  taps for selecting, confirming, and blocked actions. Off by default.
- **Game Rumble** - lets a game drive the motor. On by default. A change takes
  effect the next time you start a game.
- **Strength** - how hard the motor runs, as a percentage, for both the interface
  and games. Press **Left** and **Right** to change it in steps of 5. The device
  taps at each step so you can feel the level. The row shows a dash while both
  switches are off.

The device taps once when you turn either switch on.

## What the taps mean

With **UI Rumble** on, the number of taps tells you what happened:

- One tap - you selected something, or the cursor moved.
- Two taps - you confirmed something and it went through.
- Three taps - that action wasn't available.

**Strength** changes how hard the taps feel. The patterns stay the same.

## Rumble in games

With **Game Rumble** on, a game that supports rumble drives the motor with the
force it asks for. **Strength** caps how strong the strongest moment gets.

Game rumble works in RetroArch games, which covers most systems, and in the
standalone Dreamcast and N64 emulators. PSP and Nintendo DS games aren't covered.
